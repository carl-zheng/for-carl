# Creative Framework

Use these categories to develop concepts. Select only those that materially clarify the idea; do not turn the output into a taxonomy dump.

## Journey role

Give each concept one primary role and optional secondary roles:

- `DISCOVER`: introduce an unknown problem, product, or category
- `IDENTIFY`: make the viewer recognize that the product is for someone like them
- `EDUCATE`: explain how or why it works
- `DEMONSTRATE`: show a meaningful result
- `PROVE`: reduce disbelief
- `DIFFERENTIATE`: establish a meaningful difference
- `DESIRE`: create emotional or lifestyle pull
- `OFFER`: make the commercial proposition compelling
- `CONVERT`: remove final resistance and drive action
- `FULL-FUNNEL`: carry most of the persuasion sequence in one creative

## Product role

Choose the product's primary story function:

- `HERO`: the product itself is visually surprising
- `SOLUTION`: the problem creates demand, then the product resolves it
- `MECHANISM`: how it works is central
- `TRANSFORMATION TOOL`: it enables a visible before/after
- `DISCOVERY`: finding it is the story
- `REPLACEMENT`: it replaces an inconvenient method or object
- `PROOF OBJECT`: its use demonstrates the claim
- `DESIRE OBJECT`: its design, feel, or experience is aspirational

The opening promise and product role must connect. Do not leave the product as a late, unrelated prop.

## Tension patterns

- I want X, but the current solution causes Y.
- I believe X, but reality is Y.
- Everyone tells me X, but my experience is Y.
- The product looks like X, but actually does Y.
- I want the result, but refuse the usual sacrifice.

A tension is useful only when it is truthful and can drive a product-relevant story.

## Hook mechanisms

- audience identification
- direct problem callout
- contrarian claim
- open loop
- unexpected, product-relevant demonstration
- real social validation
- verified authority or data
- genuine offer reveal
- transformation
- skeptic-to-believer
- discovery
- confession
- comparison
- pattern interrupt tied to the product

Select hooks after the consumer truth, tension, territory, and big idea. Reject sensational openings that the body cannot repay.

## Demonstration modes

- live use
- before/after
- side-by-side comparison
- stress or speed test
- transformation
- close-up material proof
- mechanism explanation
- repeated use across scenarios
- genuine user reaction
- visual recreation of the problem
- old method versus new method

Ask: what can the viewer see that increases belief? If an important claim cannot be shown, name the evidence needed instead.

## Proof mechanisms

- product demonstration
- verified customer review or adoption evidence
- repeat purchase
- before/after evidence
- expert endorsement or certification
- guarantee
- fair comparison
- construction or ingredient evidence
- mechanism explanation
- authentic creator testimony
- multiple-user montage
- real usage footage
- press coverage
- performance data

Use only proof that exists and is approved. A proof-shaped visual is not proof.

## Offer role

Choose whether the offer is the hook, mid-story reinforcement, final conversion device, AIP-only device, or absent. Possible structures include verified discounts, free shipping, free gifts, bundles, buy-more-save-more, BOGO, trials, guarantees, or subscription benefits.

Do not manufacture urgency. Treat practitioner beliefs about a specific offer structure as test hypotheses until first-party evidence validates them.

## AIP and landing continuity

The AIP should be the next persuasive beat and reinforce the hero product, dominant promise, proof, offer, comparison, or transformation. The landing page must fulfill the expectations created by both surfaces, including product, price, bundle, discount, gift, guarantee, claims, and visual identity.

Reject a concept when its commercial promise cannot continue consistently.

## Creative Set diversity

Organize sets around a coherent theme such as audience, pain, product, use case, offer, mechanism, comparison, proof, occasion, or desire. Across concepts, change meaningful variables such as audience, tension, motivation, journey role, proof, or narrative mechanic.

Distinguish:

- meaningful variant: same product, different audience or buying motivation with corresponding proof
- superficial variant: same concept with synonymous opening copy

## Evaluation

Score each concept from 1–5 on:

1. consumer truth
2. purchase motivation
3. clarity of one dominant memory
4. hook-to-body continuity
5. product centrality
6. visible demonstration potential
7. believable proof
8. narrative depth for the proposed duration
9. video-to-AIP-to-landing continuity
10. producibility with available assets
11. strategic distinctness
12. testability and learning value

Do not let the numeric total hide a fatal flaw. Fabricated claims, broken destination continuity, or an unprovable core promise disqualify the concept.
